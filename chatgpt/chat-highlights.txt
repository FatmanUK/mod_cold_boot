Absolutely. A note before diving in: a ProTracker .MOD is a binary format containing sample data, pattern tables, and encoded pattern events. There isn't a widely adopted "source code" format analogous to assembly that can simply be converted into a valid MOD. A couple of suggestions before you start entering anything:

Save versions frequently. I recommend names like:

coldboot_00.mod
coldboot_01.mod
coldboot_02.mod

Tracker composition tends to evolve, and it's surprisingly nice to be able to go back a version or two.

Don't be afraid to tweak. Once the sample kit is in MilkyTracker, you might find that a kick wants to be 2 dB quieter, or the pad needs its loop nudged by a few samples. That's exactly how the original composers worked. They rarely treated the samples as untouchable.
Listen on headphones and speakers if you can. Four-channel MODs often balance differently depending on playback hardware, and subtle loop clicks are easier to catch on headphones.

One thing I'd really like to do once you've got the first minute playing is an old-school "composer's pass." Rather than adding more notes, we'll look for places where we can remove notes. One hallmark of the better Amiga loading tunes is that they often achieve tension through restraint. Silence, sparse harmony, and carefully placed accents can make four channels sound far bigger than they really are.
...
I hope Cold Boot ends up being one of those modules that people let play for a while before launching the game. Those were often the most memorable.
