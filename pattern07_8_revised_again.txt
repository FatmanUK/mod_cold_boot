Agreed. The seam itself probably cannot become completely invisible without rewriting Pattern 00, and that would be musical bureaucracy of the least useful kind. The realistic target is a convincing **phrase turnover**, not listener amnesia.

These revisions address the audition notes directly:

* Pattern 07 continues the exact drum-break phase established in Pattern 04.
* Bass attacks move mostly onto odd rows and use `C40`, while kick and snare levels are reduced.
* The final G-major section becomes a spacious **G7 hold**, not an arpeggiated traffic accident.

The preceding versions of Patterns 07 and 08 are discarded. These are authoritative.

One notation detail: do not add `F06` or `F91` here. These patterns inherit Speed 6 and BPM 145 from the song. This leaves the effect columns available for actual mixing, a rare victory over needless repetition.

## Why Pattern 07 is different

### Pattern 04’s break now continues

The percussion at Pattern 07 rows 00–15 has the same phase and spacing as Pattern 04 rows 48–63. The boundary no longer introduces a new drum idea.

The first bass note is delayed until row 01. That allows:

```text
Pattern 04 row 62: G-3 bass
Pattern 07 row 00: bass continues under kick
Pattern 07 row 01: D#3 bass attack
```

This should make the boundary feel like bar continuation rather than a pattern reset.

### PopBass receives its own attack space

Most bass notes now fall on odd rows:

```text
01, 05, 09, 13
17, 21, 25, 29
33, 37, 41, 45
49, 53, 57, 61
```

They also use:

```text
C40 = decimal 64
```

Kick and snare levels are reduced:

```text
C38 = 56
C34 = 52
C30 = 48
C2C = 44
C28 = 40
```

The climax should still feel forceful through density, but instruments 1 and 2 should no longer sit on the bass like large, rhythmically gifted furniture.

### The G-major section is now a G7 hold

The `047` arpeggio appears only in two-row pulses:

```text
48–49
52–53
56–57
60–61
```

It is no longer applied continuously across all sixteen rows.

The final dominant harmony consists of:

```text
Bass:       G, D, F, G
Pad:        G major, containing B
Final bell: F-5
```

That gives us the important G7 tension tones:

```text
B and F
```

The explicit final `B-4` stab is gone.

## Pattern 07 to Pattern 08 resolution

The resolution no longer depends on a lone `B-4 -> C-5` motion.


Pattern 08 begins with:


The voice leading is:

```text
B  -> C
```


while the pad handles the leading-tone movement internally.

That is a proper dominant-seventh resolution into C minor, rather than asking one rather ordinary stab to carry the emotional weight of the entire module. It had neither the training nor the temperament.
